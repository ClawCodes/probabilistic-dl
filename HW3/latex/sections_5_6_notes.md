- Section 5 - MC_RTT population activity
  - For the real-data experiment, each example was a 200-ms window centered on peak hand speed. It contained log1p Gaussian-smoothed firing rates from 48 held-in M1 units in ten 20-ms bins, and the cVAE was conditioned on one of eight peak-velocity directions.
  - I used the source-provided split of 810 training and 270 validation trials, so the reported validation results are evaluated on held-out trials rather than a random split of overlapping windows.
  - Increasing the latent space from 2 to 8 to 16 dimensions improved the final held-out reconstruction MSE from 0.877 to 0.824 to 0.766, respectively; the corresponding total validation losses were 0.885, 0.850, and 0.811.
  - The KL term increased from 4.31 to 13.48 to 24.25 nats as the latent space grew. All available dimensions were active--2/2, 8/8, and 16/16--so these models did not exhibit posterior collapse by our active-dimension criterion.
  - The training curves show that the larger models continued to lower training reconstruction error, while their validation curves flattened and rose modestly late in training. Thus, 16 dimensions gave the best final held-out reconstruction, but the curves also motivate early stopping and evaluation across additional random seeds.
  - The observed-versus-reconstructed heatmaps show that the cVAE retains broad differences across units and the overall temporal rate pattern, but it replaces sharp trial-specific peaks and troughs with smoother activity.
  - That smoothing is expected from an MSE decoder trained on already smoothed firing-rate features: it is rewarded for predicting a conditional average, rather than for reproducing every idiosyncratic fluctuation in one trial.
  - The generated mean rate profiles preserve the broad decline after peak speed in all eight direction conditions. However, the generated amplitude is systematically too low for several directions, especially 0, 1, 5, and 6, and too high for directions 2 and 3.
  - Therefore, direction conditioning controls coarse population-rate dynamics, but it does not yet reproduce the full direction-specific amplitude pattern.
  - In the PCA plot, generated samples occupy the central region of the held-out population cloud but omit many of its most extreme points. The 16-dimensional samples recover more spread than the smaller models, but all models remain less variable than the held-out data.
  - The PCA axes are only a visualization of high-dimensional variance; I do not interpret either axis, or any individual latent dimension, as a named neural variable.
  - The direction-2 sample comparison makes the same point at the unit-by-time level: the generated averages preserve coarse structure, while individual samples remain smoother and less heterogeneous than individual held-out trials.
  - Overall, I would report 16 dimensions as the best model by the final held-out reconstruction metric, while using the 8-dimensional model as the middle-capacity example for the all-direction visualizations.
- Section 6 - Limitations and conclusion
  - The main conclusion is narrow: this cVAE learned coarse, direction-conditioned structure in smoothed M1 population-rate windows, but it did not reproduce the full trial-to-trial variability of the held-out data.
  - A low reconstruction error alone is not sufficient evidence that a neural generator is realistic. The PCA and heatmap comparisons show that generated samples are underdispersed and smoother than held-out trials.
  - First, the analysis is not a full model of reaching: it uses one 200-ms window centered on peak speed from each trial, so it discards the rest of the reach and does not model transitions across time.
  - Second, the inputs are Gaussian-smoothed, log-transformed firing rates rather than raw spike counts or spike times. This makes optimization stable, but it necessarily suppresses fine temporal structure before the VAE sees the data.
  - Third, movement was represented by an eight-level direction label. Continuous velocity, target position, and other task variables could explain variation that the latent distribution currently must absorb.
  - Fourth, this result comes from 48 selected held-in units in one recording session and one train/validation split. It does not establish generalization across sessions, animals, unit selections, seeds, or hyperparameter settings.
  - The late validation rise for the larger models also suggests that future fits should select checkpoints by validation performance rather than automatically using the final epoch.
  - A stronger next model would generate full reach-aligned sequences with a temporal VAE or state-space model, condition on continuous kinematics or target displacement, and use a count-aware likelihood such as Poisson or negative binomial rather than an MSE Gaussian decoder.
  - A stronger evaluation would compare generated and held-out population covariance, pairwise correlations, firing-rate distributions, and the accuracy of a kinematic decoder trained or tested on generated samples.
  - Thus, the latent-dimension sweep shows that added capacity helps held-out reconstruction and remains active, but visual sample evaluation shows that capacity alone does not solve the fidelity problem.
